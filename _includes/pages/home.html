The numbers changed. Again.

D65 parents caught a $27 million mistake.

$17M → $27.35M

The error D65 first confirmed, then the full five-year figure. Building costs were counted twice.[1]

$2.3M a year

Revised savings from closing both Dewey and Lincolnwood, down from $2.9M in October. About $2M once added busing is counted.[1]

3 errors in 3 weeks

Building costs counted twice. A required $3.2M yearly payment for Foster left out of the budget. Closure savings overstated. All flagged by parents.[1]

Do the math

Fixing D65’s math is worth about as much as closing both schools.

The corrected projection improves D65’s outlook by about $2.3 million a year, even after adding back the Foster payment the district left out. That’s about the same amount closing Dewey and Lincolnwood would save.[1]

≈ The correction alone is about as much as the projected closure savings.

Per-year averages over the five-year projection (FY2028 to FY2032). The $27.35M error averages $5.47M a year, and the Foster lease payment is $3.2M a year, leaving $2.27M—rounded to $2.3M. With added busing costs, the closures save about $2M a year, so the correction is worth slightly more. This comparison is a calculation from reported figures, not new revenue or a claim that the district’s financial challenges are resolved. [1]

Who checked the model?

D65 invited two parents with finance careers to review its plan. After about three hours with the CFO on November 3, six days before the vote, neither offered an assurance that the model had received the detailed review needed.[1]

“there’s not enough time to really do the kind of detailed review that I think you would need to do”[1]

— Kerrie Dvorak, Lincolnwood parent and accounting executive

The audit isn’t done

The independent audit of FY2026 finances is not yet complete. Parent and CPA Ben Van Laan told the RoundTable that the CFO said it was still months away; Van Laan said it was an important input for establishing a baseline. A board member asked for independent review of the projections on October 26.[1]

A private meeting six days before the vote isn’t that review.

If parents keep finding errors, how many are still in there?

[1] Source: district slides and interviews, as reported by the Evanston RoundTable, November 5, 2026. The approximate comparison and call for independent review are For a Brighter Evanston’s analysis and position.
