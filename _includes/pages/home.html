Una factura de $600 millones en reparaciones que no se sostiene

La administración dice que el D65 necesita casi $600 millones en reparaciones de edificios, y el plan actual da esa cifra por sentada. Una madre o un padre que examinó los propios datos del Distrito encontró:

Cuenta escuelas que ya están cerradas. Kingsley y Bessie Rhodes siguen en la lista de reparaciones.

A $600 million repair bill that doesn't hold up

The administration says D65 needs nearly $600 million in building repairs, and the current plan treats that number as a given. A parent who dug into the District's own data found:

It counts schools that are already closed. Kingsley and Bessie Rhodes are still on the repair list.

It assumes the most expensive heating and cooling option. That's close to $100 million for geothermal. A conventional heating and cooling system would likely cost less. Federal tax credits could also lower the geothermal cost, but it's unclear whether the District's estimate accounts for them.

Some costs are counted twice. The plan pays for new geothermal systems and for some of the older heating and cooling upgrades they would replace. Other items, like galvanized pipe replacements, are counted twice too.

About $130 million could be paid for with special safety bonds, not the regular budget. When the Evanston RoundTable sent the District detailed questions for this story, the administration didn't answer a single one.

El gasto en reparaciones es una elección

  • Meta actual$2.7Mpolítica actual del Distrito
  • Opción de las familias$4.7Mmenos del doble de la meta actual
  • Plan del Distrito$8.3Mmás del triple de la meta actual

Como dijo la presidenta de la Junta, la cantidad que el D65 gasta en reparaciones es «una elección». Hoy, la meta del Distrito es de $2.7 millones al año. El plan de la administración aumentaría esa cifra a más del triple, hasta $8.3 millones.

Las familias demostraron que aumentarla a $4.7 millones en su lugar, que sigue siendo un aumento considerable, mantiene abiertas Dewey y Lincolnwood y deja al Distrito en una situación financiera aproximadamente igual, sin cambiar nada más en el plan. Eso da tiempo para hacer esto bien: dar a la comunidad una elección clara sobre un referéndum y dejar que la próxima persona a cargo de la superintendencia corrija las cifras para que tengan sentido.

Repair spending is a choice

  • Today's goal$2.7Mcurrent District policy
  • Parents' option$4.7Mless than double today's goal
  • District plan$8.3Mmore than triple today's goal

As the board president put it, the amount D65 spends on repairs is "a choice." Today, the District's goal is $2.7 million a year. The administration's plan would more than triple that, to $8.3 million.

Parents showed that raising it to $4.7 million instead, still a big increase, keeps Dewey and Lincolnwood open and leaves the District in about the same financial shape, without changing anything else in the plan. That buys time to do this right: give the community a clear choice on a referendum, and let the next superintendent fix the numbers so they make sense.
