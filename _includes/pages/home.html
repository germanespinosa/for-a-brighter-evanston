D65 needs a referendum. Voters aren’t there yet.

Every plan that keeps D65 on solid ground for the long term includes a referendum, the district’s included. An independent poll of 665 Evanston voters shows they aren’t ready to say yes, and closing schools pushes them further away.

41%

would oppose a referendum today. Only 28% would support it; 31% are unsure.

Nearly 4 to 1

Closing Dewey and Lincolnwood makes voters less likely to vote yes, not more: 41% to 11%.

65%

oppose D65’s original October 13 budget plan, including SACC and Head Start cuts. Only 21% support it.

It’s a band-aid, not a plan.

Closing Dewey and Lincolnwood saves about $2.3 million a year by the district’s own latest estimate, about 1% of D65’s budget. In our corrected, extended model of the district’s projections, reserves run out in FY34 instead of FY33. That’s one year, in exchange for two schools, new boundaries, new bus routes, learning loss and a massive amount of disruption.

And the plan rests on numbers that keep moving. Parents caught a $27 million error, and the forecast changed three times in three weeks. Don’t vote on a plan built on a $27 million error.

There are better options. Let’s work on them together.

Our financial model starts from the district’s own projections and fixes the errors parents found. It shows a path that keeps all schools open and keeps reserves above the board’s 90-day safety line every year through 2037. It has room to spare, so it still holds even if some pieces fall short.

Spend carefully

Create an efficient central office and prioritize student-facing positions. Engage in honest and fair conversations about compensation and benefits.

Use what already exists

Sell the Kingsley and Bessie Rhodes properties and work with the city to return TIF money to schools.

Ask voters, with a plan they trust

A 2028 referendum similar to the one Evanston passed in 2017, asked only after a long-term plan with spending limits and independent oversight is in place.

Don’t take our word for it. Review the model, explore the assumptions, and check the math. These are model projections, contingent on the proposed measures and assumptions, not guaranteed outcomes.
