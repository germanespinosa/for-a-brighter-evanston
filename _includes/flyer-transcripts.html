A QUIÉNES GOLPEA MÁS ESTE PLAN

  1. Familias de TWI: tercera pérdida seguida
    Dewey tiene un programa de inmersión bilingüe en español (TWI). El D65 ya cerró Bessie Rhodes y terminó el programa TWI de Willard. Ahora las familias de TWI de Dewey también serían desplazadas.
  2. Estudiantes de STEP: rutinas rotas
    El programa STEP de Lincolnwood atiende a estudiantes con autismo que dependen de salones, personal y rutinas conocidos. El plan traslada todo el programa a otra escuela, y el costo de esa mudanza no está en el presupuesto.

REVISA LAS CUENTAS DEL PLAN DEL DISTRITO

  1. No podemos confiar en los números
    El D65 reconoció un error de pronóstico de $27 millones. Sus propios consultores luego encontraron otro error de $7.3 millones. Las necesidades de reparación están infladas y se omiten costos reales.
  2. Ganancias mínimas
    Cerrar Dewey y Lincolnwood ahorra unos $2.3 millones al año antes de los costos de transporte, de $21 millones en recortes.
  3. Solo compra un año
    Cerrar Dewey y Lincolnwood le da al distrito un año más. Luego vuelve el mismo déficit, porque cerrar escuelas no arregla lo que lo causa.
  4. Ignora los ahorros más grandes
    Un aumento salarial en línea con distritos cercanos, una oficina central más pequeña, vender edificios ya cerrados y recuperar el dinero de los TIF ahorrarían mucho más.

La verdadera disciplina fiscal es un plan a largo plazo. Hay opciones que lo logran con ambas escuelas abiertas. Míralas en forabrighterevanston.org y firma el compromiso.

Kids carry the cost.

The closures are not final. Before the board takes its deciding vote, it needs to hear what this plan would do to children. Here is who is affected, and how you can help.

WHO THIS PLAN HITS HARDEST

  1. TWI families: a third loss in a row
    Dewey is home to a Spanish Two-Way Immersion program. D65 already closed Bessie Rhodes and ended Willard’s TWI program. Now Dewey’s TWI families would be uprooted too.
  2. STEP students: routines uprooted
    Lincolnwood’s STEP program serves students with autism who rely on familiar rooms, staff and routines. The plan moves the whole program to another school, and the cost of that move is not in the budget.
  3. Nearly 650 children starting over
    Each would lose their school, teachers and community. Many more children would ride a bus or walk long distances to get to school.

There are other ways to fix the budget. Closing schools is being sold as fiscal discipline. It’s a false fix. It covers about a tenth of the cuts, and the shortfall comes right back. The district has other choices that balance the budget and keep both schools open.

Closing schools is being sold as fiscal discipline. It’s a false fix.

The closures are not final yet. D65’s plan is a band-aid. It covers the wound and leaves the cause untreated.

CHECK THE MATH ON THE DISTRICT’S PLAN

  1. We can’t trust the numbers
    D65 has acknowledged a $27 million forecasting error. Its own consultants then found another $7.3 million error. Building repair needs are overstated, and real costs are left out.
  2. Minimal gains
    Closing Dewey and Lincolnwood saves about $2.3 million a year before busing costs, out of $21 million in cuts.
  3. It buys us one year
    Closing Dewey and Lincolnwood buys the district one more year. Then the same shortfall is back, because closing schools does not fix what is driving it.
  4. It skips the bigger savings
    Salary growth in line with nearby districts, a leaner central office, selling closed buildings and returning TIF money would save far more.

Real fiscal discipline is a long-term plan. There are options that get there with both schools open. See them at forabrighterevanston.org and sign the pledge.
