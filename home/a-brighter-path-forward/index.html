Path forward · Financial model

Closing two schools buys one year. There’s a better path.

We rebuilt D65’s budget projections from the district’s own numbers. Our model shows a path that keeps schools open and keeps reserves above the board’s 90-day safety line each projected year from FY28 through FY37.

See the options ↓ Executive summary (PDF)

The bottom line

  • Closing Dewey and Lincolnwood saves about $2.3 million a year, about 1% of the district’s roughly $180 million budget. That’s the district’s latest estimate, down from $2.9 million in October, and added busing brings it closer to $2 million. Our model also adds the costs of about 14 additional positions that the district identified as dependent on the school closures on October 26.
  • In our corrected, extended model of the district’s projections, the closures buy one year. Reserves run out in FY34 with the closures and FY33 without them. The structural gap stays. We counted the closure savings generously ($3.3 million a year, including about 14 positions the district tied to the closures), and they still buy only one year.
  • There’s another route. A package of savings and revenue keeps schools open and keeps reserves above the board’s 90-day policy each projected year from FY28 through FY37.
  • Our ask: vote no on Nov. 16, and work through these options in public before any building closes.

Why a referendum, and why a plan comes first

Every path that keeps D65 above the safety line for the long term includes a referendum, the district’s plan included. An independent poll shows a referendum trails today (28% for, 41% against, 31% unsure), and closing schools makes it harder to pass. Voters said what would change their minds: a long-term financial plan with clear spending limits. Our model shows how to give them that first, then ask.

How we built the model

We started from the district’s projections (dated Aug. 26, released Oct. 13), which run through FY32, and extended each line to FY37 at its FY32 growth rate. We looked at what savings would be reversed from the district’s Interventions scenario if both schools were left open, which included the district’s $2.3 million in claimed savings plus some additional positions identified on October 26, to create three total scenarios: Base (No Action), Interventions, and Both Schools Open. Then we made a few corrections in every scenario.

  • Capital spending: we replaced projected capital outlay plus transfers with the model’s $7,967,225 annual capital assumption (Inputs row 43), subject to its Base FY28 exception. This is not the same as the reported $27.35M five-year correction for double-counted capital spending. The annual amount includes both outlay and transfers; it is not new revenue.
  • Foster lease debt: we added $3.2M a year the projections left out. This adds cost.
  • Missing revenue: we added the Ryan Field ticket surcharge ($250,000 a year from FY28) and Five-Fifths TIF revenue sharing (about $358,000 a year).
  • SACC: kept in every scenario, as D65 decided on Nov. 5.

Fiscal years end in June (FY34 is the 2033–34 school year). The board’s policy is to keep at least 90 days of spending in reserve.

What we’re upfront about

  • The referendum, TIF changes, building sales, salary and benefit growth, and retirement savings are our proposals and estimates, not approved district figures. The building sale is a one-time step to bridge to 2028 in place of borrowing, and most of it (about $8 million of $9 million) could cover building costs the district now pays from operations.
  • We have open questions for the district, including what busing students from closed schools would cost (we estimate $240,000 a year) and whether the 41 closure-related positions are cut or moved.
  • The model works on yearly balances, so it can’t show cash timing within a year.
  • These are model projections, not audited or guaranteed outcomes. Results depend on the assumptions and proposed measures being achieved.
