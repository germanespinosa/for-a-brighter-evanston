Voter poll · 665 Evanston voters · Nov. 2026

D65 is counting on a referendum. Voters aren’t there yet.

Public Policy Polling surveyed 665 registered Evanston voters. A referendum trails in this poll: 28% support, 41% oppose and 31% are unsure. Closing schools makes it harder to pass. Here’s everything they said.

Poll executive summary (PDF, FABE) Full results and question wording (PDF, PPP)

Three things voters made clear

  1. 01

    A referendum trails today.

    28% would support one, 41% would oppose it and 31% are unsure. 47% say local property taxes are already too high, compared to 38% who say they’re willing to pay more for local schools.

  2. 02

    Closing schools makes it worse.

    41% say closing Dewey and Lincolnwood makes them less likely to vote yes. 11% say more likely; 28% say no difference and 20% are unsure.

  3. 03

    Voters want a plan first.

    66% want the board to hold off on closing schools until it has a fuller long-term financial and academic plan. 19% want it to move ahead now; 15% are unsure. The top thing that would earn their support is a long-term financial plan with clear spending limits (38%), more than double the next answer (an independent citizen committee overseeing spending, 18%).

Voters oppose D65’s original plan

65% oppose D65’s original budget plan and 21% support it. Opposition outweighs support in every group the poll measured, including D65 parents (78% oppose), homeowners (64%) and voters over 65 (53%).

Q4 described the original October 13 plan, including ending SACC and Head Start. These responses do not measure support for the subsequently revised plan.

This isn’t just parents

78% of the voters polled have no child in a D65 school. They oppose the plan 62% to 23%, and want the board to hold off on closures 62% to 20%.

Voters’ top priorities for the school board

Improving academic achievement comes first (33%), then keeping neighborhood schools open (24%). Balancing the budget is third (19%).

Voters said what would earn their support. Here’s a path to get there.

The board can’t win a referendum by closing schools first and asking for trust later. Voters want a long-term financial plan with spending limits. Our financial model shows one way there: it keeps both schools open and keeps D65 above the board’s 90-day safety line each projected year from FY28 through FY37, under its proposed-package assumptions.

How the poll was done

Public Policy Polling surveyed 665 registered Evanston voters on Nov. 4 and 5, 2026, by text (95%) and landline (5%), as recorded in Q26. The supplied FABE executive summary reports that respondents were drawn at random from voter rolls and results weighted to reflect the electorate; it reports a full-sample margin of error of ±3.8 percentage points. Subgroup margins are wider.

What the numbers do and don’t tell us

A poll is a snapshot of what voters think right now. It can’t tell the board how to close the deficit. What it adds is a clear picture of what voters support, what they oppose and what would earn their trust. We said we’d share the results no matter what they showed. Every number is linked above. Percentages are rounded and may not total 100%.
