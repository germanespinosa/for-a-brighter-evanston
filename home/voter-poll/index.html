A referendum would fail today.

28% would support one and 41% would oppose it. 47% say local property taxes are already too high, compared to 38% who say they’re willing to pay more for local schools.

02
4 to 1

Closing schools makes it worse.

41% say closing Dewey and Lincolnwood makes them less likely to vote yes. Only 11% say more likely.

03
66%

Voters want a plan first.

66% want the board to hold off on closing schools until it has a long-term plan. 19% want it to move ahead now.

65% oppose D65's original budget plan and 21% support it. Opposition outweighs support in every group the poll measured, including D65 parents (78% oppose), homeowners (64%) and voters over 65 (53%).

A brighter path forward

Voters said what would earn their support. Here’s a path to get there.

The board can’t win a referendum by closing schools first and asking for trust later. Voters want a long-term financial plan with spending limits. Our financial model shows one way there: it keeps both schools open and keeps D65 above the board’s 90-day safety line every year through 2037.

Methodology

How the poll was done

Public Policy Polling surveyed 665 registered Evanston voters on Nov. 4 and 5, 2026, by text (95%) and landline (5%). Respondents were chosen at random from Evanston’s voter rolls, not self-selected, and results are weighted to reflect the city’s electorate.

Read with care

What the numbers do and don’t tell us

A poll is a snapshot of what voters think right now. It can’t tell the board how to close the deficit. What it adds is a clear picture of what voters support, what they oppose and what would earn their trust. We said we’d share the results no matter what they showed. Every number is linked above.

Who paid for this poll

For a Brighter Evanston commissioned this poll, and Public Policy Polling, an independent firm, conducted it. The cost was raised from neighbors on GoFundMe. We’ll post a full accounting, with receipts, in the coming days. The complete question wording is linked at the top of this page.
